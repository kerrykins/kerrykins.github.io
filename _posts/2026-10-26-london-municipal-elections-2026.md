## Voting Matters

In 2022, only ~25% of voters chose the mayor and the city's councillors. The record low turnout handed a small group of people a disproportionate amount of power over a city of more than 400,000.

**At the same time, lower turnout means that ballots carry greater weight.** London's Ward 13 race in the 2022 election **came down to 34 votes.** That councillor now represents 20,000 people. 

City council shapes your daily life. They control our roads, water, buses (many of us have qualms about LTC and the BRT), municipal bylaws, and development in our communities. 

Municipal by-law can affect workers directly. Municipalities have tried passing by-laws to allow retail businesses to open on most public holidays; this can strip up to eight paid days off from retail workers. Region of York passed such a bylaw with no public consultation. Toronto and Brampton have tried as well. 

Your voice matters.

### Claims & Jurisdictions

**Jurisdiction matters.** Some candidates' claims do not reflect what the city can *actually* directly control. For instance, municipal officials can only advocate for health care as health-care delivery is provincial. On the other hand, claims such as "I'll cut property taxes" is firmly within city council's power. 

Other issues are not so clear-cut. For instance, for housing, the city controls zoning, development proposals, and what to do on city land. However, the province manages the landlord-tenant law and sets housing targets. Significant transfers are made from the federal government. 

City council is responsible for things like property-taxes, zoning, planning, roads and urban infrastructure maintenance, recreational facilities, sanitary, size of police budgets, local by-laws, and London Transit funding and service levels.

## Issues

### About Transit 

In 2026, the city has voted to return to a board of seven with four councillors and three citizen appointments (still in the air). Regardless, the elected officials you vote on have a direct effect on London Transit. 

Some of the gaps are the unfinished, scaled-down, and budget-overridden BRT network, unclaimed federal and provincial transfers, frequency issues, fare affordability, paratransit, and poor urban planning where bus routes are established *long after* a subdivision has been built. 

46% of riders in 2025 were satisfied with its on-time performance. In 2026, LTC's average on-time performance was 78.9% with September sitting at 63%. 

Read more: https://londondecides.ca/files/transit/

### About Schools

__London is home to four public school boards.__ Note that the largest, Thames Valley District School Board (TVDSB) is [under provincial control due to an investigation](https://www.ontario.ca/page/thames-valley-district-school-board-financial-investigation); its trustees have reduced power. 

The trustee ballot is the most overlooked ballot. Consider reading up on trustee's positions. EDI policies and position report cards are included two sections below. 

School board trustees decide the school board's budget, where schools are located, leadership hiring, school calendar, *codes of conduct*, and busing. 

For the LGBTQ+ community, because a school board trustee has power over codes of conduct, voting is important in protecting the rights and safety of queer students.

## Getting Ready to Vote 

### Voter Notification Card 

You should have been sent a **voter notification card** to your registered address already. 

The card contains your assigned voting location. You cannot choose another voting location. You can still add your name to the Voters’ List in person on Voting Day, Monday, October 26, 2026.

### Students 

If you are a student living in London to attend Fanshawe College or Western University, you can update your information to your current London address. 

Note that while the deadline to update your Voters’ List information online has passed. You can still add your name to the Voters’ List in person on Voting Day, Monday, October 26, 2026.

*You will be able to vote in London AND in your home municipality this election.*

**Western University** - [you can update your address and generate a letter with your local address through Western Votes.](https://westernvotes.uwo.ca/)

### Accessible Voting

If you find voting by paper ballot difficult, you can bring a friend or relative to help you mark the ballot.  When you arrive at the poll, be sure to tell the greeter you have someone assisting you.

[Accessible Elections for London Ontario](https://london.ca/government/council-civic-administration/elections/accessible-elections)
  
### Identification

Identification is required when you vote. 

You will need to show ID which **contains your name and address**. You may have to show two pieces of ID to accomplish this. Photo ID is not needed. A utility bill or driver's license works. 

See [here](https://london.ca/government/council-civic-administration/elections/information-voters) for a list of accepted identification.

### Ward Map

When reviewing candidates, [double check the ward](https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/81a926b75cb04f5e81892267a5fe79c8) you belong in.
